## 19. Canonical setting synopsis

**Status:** Established canon summarized from the supplied reference. Read all dates at the same approximate precision as the chronology.

Prospect was settled centuries ago by the passengers of nine generation ships, carrying seven sapient humanoid peoples from a single home star system: the ancestral **Kell**, **Drask** and **Lirra**; the hybrid-descended **Revari**; and the engineered **Astrel**, **Voldren** and **Selk** (all seven names currently working selections). The engineered peoples are also known by historical occupational labels: **Void Walkers**, **Cave Crawlers** and **Sea Walkers**, respectively. The engineered peoples were legally considered property when the expedition departed, although emancipation movements had already emerged.

The home civilization had previously experienced a catastrophic crisis involving advanced AI, leaving a lasting distrust of autonomous machine intelligence and contributing to its reliance on engineered biological labor. Also before departure, two Kell scientists identified the **Venn–Saye field**, an exotic physical phenomenon whose early applications were limited. After colonists encountered Prospect's indigenous biology, their descendants developed sophisticated field-based technologies and the genetically specialized practice of **Dynamicism**. Today ordinary people operate engineered Venn–Saye devices through **Fieldworks**, while **Dynamicists** (*Dynos*) use a universal **catalyst** and extensive school-specific genetic alterations for advanced field manipulation. No Dynamicist can survive specializing in more than one school.

Upon arriving at Prospect, the Nine established separate settlements using the remains of their ships. Over generations, protected transportation corridors enabled trade, migration, and cultural integration. A political unification movement eventually brought the settlements together as Haven.

Haven grew into an enormous protected metropolis. When the expansion of its environmental protection system reached its practical horizontal limits, construction turned upward, creating layers of urban development over the original ship settlements.

Over subsequent centuries, independent orbital habitats were founded by populations seeking alternatives to Haven's political, economic, and cultural institutions. These became collectively known as the Constellation, beginning with **Elsewhere**. Founded approximately 400 years before the present, Elsewhere is now a roughly **30-km-long, 2-km-diameter rotating cylinder** with a huge axial light and a stationary docking complex at one end. Its interior is a single broad open space whose oldest, densest districts lie dockward; increasingly spacious development extends toward agricultural districts at the far end. It is a representative civic republic founded on liberty, shared civic institutions and equal rights for all seven peoples, and a diversified commercial center with broad introductory services and training.

Approximately 150 years before the present, indigenous alien life breached Haven's environmental defenses. The resulting Bloom spread through the city's interconnected systems, while escalating automated containment measures isolated districts and accelerated the collapse. Haven was abandoned, and the already-established orbital societies chose not to rebuild it.

Today, the Constellation is the center of civilization in the Prospect system. Its seven major habitats maintain distinct identities and often competing interests, and each is insular, self-sufficient and self-governing, with no Constellation-wide law, body or standing agreement among them. Besides Elsewhere they are **Freehold** (worker-owned heavy industry), **Meridian** (trade and finance, rich and unequal), **Anchorage** (Haven's former orbital port, crowded with the evacuees' descendants and claiming Haven's succession), **Lantern** (a research charter that alone permits controlled machine-autonomy research), **Tailings** (an unchartered cluster of old processing platforms) and **Hermitage** (ascetic and self-sufficient, sealed for about a century after the Bloom). Smaller stations lie between them. Meanwhile the planet below remains a biologically rich, dangerous world supporting resource extraction, exploration, and salvage. Haven's immense layered ruins preserve centuries of history beneath the encroaching alien biosphere. The initial game prototype, located in a small area of Elsewhere, is explicitly a **conversation-focused experiment**: approximately five or six rooms, three proposed LLM-driven characters, other simple ambient residents and curated interaction logs for potential future LoRA fine-tuning. Full simulated economy, travel, combat and autonomous social life are deferred.
