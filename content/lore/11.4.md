Meridian grew around the Constellation's principal freight transfer hub,
the point through which trade between Haven and the habitats, and later
among the habitats themselves, was routed and financed. It became the
Constellation's financial and commercial centre. After the Bloom, its
shipping and trading houses reorganised the Constellation's trade around
the habitats instead of Haven, which secured Meridian's position for
good.

Meridian's government is strongly shaped by commercial interests: its
great trading houses hold seats in its governing body by right,
alongside elected members.

Meridian is the richest habitat and the most unequal. Fortunes are made
and lost there and wealth is worn openly. The Constellation's finest
goods, imports and luxuries pass through it, and so do people who
arrived with nothing and work its docks and warehouses. The legal
equality of the seven peoples holds on Meridian, but its old trading
houses are disproportionately ancestral-descended, and the engineered
peoples are over-represented on its docks and under-represented in its
boardrooms, a pattern Meridian's politics treats as a private matter
rather than a public one.

Physically, Meridian is not one planned structure but a cluster: the old
transfer hub at its heart, ringed and layered over the centuries with
docks, warehouses, exchanges and spun residential sections of every age,
linked by transit lines.

Elsewherians think of Meridian as sharp, fast and money-minded, the place
to go for anything Elsewhere does not stock, and watch its traders with
a mixture of respect and wariness. Meridian's traders think of Elsewhere
as a large, steady, slightly dull market. The two habitats trade heavily
and quarrel about duties.
