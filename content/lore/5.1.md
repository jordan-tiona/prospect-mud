The Kell are furred, predominantly canine-looking, digitigrade
humanoids, with functional hands, expressive, mobile ears and
individual variation in coats and proportions. They are not literal
Earth dogs or wolves.

Build: rangy, around 2 m tall and about 65 kg on average, with long
legs, a narrow chest, light bones, a long neck and a short torso; the
lean build is simply their shape. They stand with the other mid-height
peoples; the Voldren are the shortest people and the Astrel the
tallest.

Head: a medium-long, narrow muzzle ending in a dry, ridged nose plate
with slit nostrils that can close; there is no wet nose. Eyes have
visible whites and round pupils, with a reflective layer behind the
retina, so in a dim room a Kell's eyes catch light at an angle and
flash. Ears are large, erect and rigid, with thin leathery skin and
little or no fur, translucent at the edge and darkening with heat or
exertion; other peoples habitually read them.

A ruff of longer guard hairs runs from the crown down the spine, 25
to 30 cm at the crown and nape, tapering to about 10 cm at the small
of the back. Lying flat it drapes down the back; raised, it stands as
a crest from skull to shoulders, and it lifts with strong emotion or
alarm. The tail is full length, carried low and relaxed, lifting as a
mood tell, with a milder crest of 6 to 8 cm hairs along its top.

Hands have four fingers and a thumb, blunt non-retracting claws kept
filed short, and leathery pads on the fingertips and the heel of the
palm. Feet are digitigrade, padded and always bare; light cloth wraps
are casual wear and thicker padded wraps are worn for work. Kell do
not wear shoes.

Coats come in slate, blue-grey, russet and cream, dappled or
brindled, and grey around the muzzle and ears with age.

Most Kell wear no top. When they do, it is a backless, sleeveless
garment between a dress and an apron: a front panel held by strips of
cloth over the shoulders and tied at the hips or lower back, leaving
the spine and the ruff uncovered.

The two original discoverers of the Venn-Saye field were Kell. Their
scientific and educational institutions developed an especially long
tradition of Venn-Saye dynamics, preserved across the voyage and
subsequent settlement, and many Kell communities regard this as an
important cultural achievement.
