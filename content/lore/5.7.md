The Selk were engineered for hazardous underwater construction,
deep-water extraction, salvage and related work. Their historical or
colloquial designation is Sea Walkers. Selk are air-breathing and do
not need submerged accommodations; they lived aboard the generation
ships across generations without flooded compartments. Their aquatic
adaptations are not immunity to Prospect's ocean hazards, and they
are not restricted to aquatic professions.

They are mid-height, 1.9 to 2.0 m and 85 to 95 kg on average, dense
swimming muscle with a deep chest. Skin is smooth, hairless and cool
to the touch, needing no water to stay healthy, countershaded dark
above and pale below in slate blue, dark green or grey-brown over
cream; scars heal white and stay visible, and the skin pales with
age. Their looks point to none of the three ancestral peoples in
particular.

Head: broad and slightly flattened, with a short wide muzzle and a
wide mouth, teeth hidden at rest, and no dorsal fin. Eyes are set
wide, with visible whites and a protective inner lid that sweeps
across while swimming. There are no external ears, only small
openings, and the nostrils seal shut, closing reflexively when
startled. A line of fine sensory pits along the jaw and down each
flank reads pressure and movement in water, and faintly in air, so a
Selk tends to notice someone moving behind them before anyone else
does.

Fin-like stabilising appendages run along the outer forearms and
calves, folded flat against the limb on land and spread in water.
Hands have four fingers and a thumb with mild webbing; the
plantigrade feet are very fully webbed, so ordinary shoes do not fit
and Selk wear footwear made for them. They have no tail. They hold
their breath and store oxygen exceptionally well, and tolerate
pressure at depth.

Selk dress in simple fitted clothing, often sleeveless, in
water-shedding fabrics, cut to let the fins lie flat, and dislike
heavy layers and anything that would drag in water.
